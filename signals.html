What these bots are

In the big Discord rooms these channels are usually not the trader's own work. They are white-label bots from a third-party data vendor (Tradytics is a common one) that post into the room automatically, around the clock.

BotWhat one alert showsWhat it leaves out
Stock breakoutsTicker, hourly or daily chart, "momentum" score, relative volume, the resistance (or support) line, a chartEntry, stop, size, earnings date, market context, how often such breakouts fail, what happened afterwards
Dark pool printsA large off-exchange trade: ticker, size, price, notionalWhether it was a buy or a sell (prints carry no side), that many are delayed or are hedges and index trades, any follow-up
Options sweepsAn order split across exchanges to fill fast: strike, expiry, premium, "bullish/bearish"Whether it opened or closed a position, whether it hedges stock, and how sweeps in this name usually turn out
Unusual flowOption volume well above open interest, premium spentThe same gaps, plus that "unusual" is common: hundreds of names a day qualify

Why a room runs them, and what value they really add

Value to the room

  • The room never looks empty. Dozens of posts a day, at night and at weekends, for a small monthly vendor fee.
  • The subscription feels bigger. "Scanners and flow included", tools that cost money elsewhere.
  • "Smart money" is a strong story. Flow and dark-pool alerts suggest a peek behind the curtain.
  • Never wrong. "Potential breakout", no entry, no stop, no follow-up: no alert can be shown to have failed.

Value to the member

  • Saves scanning time. A ready watchlist of names that are moving, with a level drawn.
  • Something to do. A beginner gets ideas to look at instead of a blank screen.
  • Pattern exposure. Seeing hundreds of breakout charts trains the eye a little.
  • But: no way to tell the good alerts from the noise, no plan, and no feedback. Beginners tend to chase the alert after the move.
Honest summary: the value is convenience and engagement, not proven predictive power. Anyone with market data can build these bots; Claude Code can build a breakout bot of the same quality in about a day. The scarce thing is the answer key: what happened to every alert, and why.

So KAT won't present any of these alerts as calls. It presents them as practice material, with the outcome attached.

The KAT version: same alert, eight additions

01 · RECORD

Every alert scored

Next day, 5 days, 20 days: did it hold the level, the best run-up, the worst drawdown. A public scoreboard by alert type and score band, failures included.

02 · PLAN

A plan card

Trigger level, what invalidates it (a close back below the line), stop distance in ATR, size for 1 % risk on a sample account, and the reward needed to be worth it.

03 · CONTEXT

Warnings

Earnings within days, market trend and volatility, sector, how stretched the price is, a gap. Flow alerts show whether the trade looks like a hedge, a close or an opening.

04 · FEWER

Ranked, not flooded

Five best per day in one digest before the open, with the reason each made the list; the full list stays one click away.

05 · LESSON

Why it's a breakout

An annotated chart and a two-minute lesson, then "would you take it?" before the outcome is shown.

06 · PRACTICE

One click to the simulator

Take the alert with its plan in the simulator; the learner's result sits beside the alert's own.

07 · REVIEW

Weekly post-mortem

"Last week's 25 breakouts: 9 held, 16 failed. What the failures had in common." Built from the record, not from memory.

08 · ASK KAT

History on demand

"Show me every breakout like this one in 15 years and what happened next", answered with a chart.
